## 1. Review objective

Verify that Max's decision on `mixed` impact is accurately propagated: when the same behavior has both positive and negative impact on the same dimension, record the positive and negative 1–5 impact degrees separately to preserve their relative strengths; introduce no percentages, net values, or single overall intensity.

## 2. First-round findings and handling

### High: per-side evidence and revision lifecycle insufficient

Fixed:

- `mixed`'s positive and negative directions always store evidence and rationale separately, even when one side's impact degree is `null`.
- Both sides store rationale, confidence, and confirmation status separately.
- Distinguish "impact degree unknown/cleared" from "direction rejected".
- When only one side is rejected, the valid direction becomes single-direction on the other side; when both sides are rejected, the whole entry does not enter long-term analysis.
- The original candidate, direction transitions, and re-confirmations all retain revision history.

### Medium: formal conflict between Decision 004 and Decision 005

Fixed: Decision 005 explicitly supersedes Decision 004's TBD clause locally on the `mixed` impact-degree structure; Decision 004's other rules remain in force.

### Medium: comparison context not enforceable

Fixed: impact assessments store the assessment time window, Blueprint version, Goal context version, and scale anchor version; direct comparison across behaviors, across dimensions, or after time-window or context-version changes is prohibited.

### Medium: AI numeric anchoring and judgment ownership

Fixed:

- AI numbers must be labeled as rough candidates, not measurements, not comparable across entries.
- Users can hide the AI's impact degree and judge for themselves first, or choose "uncertain".
- The POC measures AI-number acceptance/modification bias, perceived autonomy, and feeling judged; complying with the AI is not a goal.

### Medium: subjective impact degree treated as objective accuracy

Fixed: an exact 1–5 oracle is set only when the Capture explicitly provides the user's self-rating, or Max provides a personal baseline in advance; other cases assess whether `null` is correctly maintained, acceptable ranges, calibration, and revision cost.

## 3. Second-round suggestions and handling

1. UAT examples could still have demanded unfounded exact 1–5 candidates. Now clarified: without self-rating or personal baseline, the impact degree stays `null` pending confirmation.
2. Added AIX-014: context updates must produce a new immutable version identifier, retain old versions, and reject cross-version comparison, differencing, or merged trends.

## 4. Final verification

The independent final re-review confirmed:

- Per-side impact degree, evidence, rationale, confidence, and status propagate consistently.
- `null`, clearing, single-side rejection, both-side rejection, re-confirmation, and direction-transition semantics are distinguishable.
- Subtraction, division, percentage-ization, netting, cross-context comparison, and Life Score conversion are prohibited.
- 1–5 remains a subjective ordered estimate, not described as objective measurement or causal fact.
- User confirmation does not upgrade an AI inference into direct fact.
- Subjective oracles, anchoring safeguards, autonomy metrics, and stop conditions have entered testing.
- v0.4 historical files were not overwritten; v0.5 continues as `In Review`.
- G1 not approved, coding not authorized.
- `git diff --check`, Markdown fences, JSON examples, and local link checks passed.
